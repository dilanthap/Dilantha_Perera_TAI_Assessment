# Northwind Financial Partners — Acceptable Use of Artificial Intelligence

**Policy reference:** NFP-IT-014
**Version:** 2.1
**Effective:** 1 March 2025
**Owner:** Office of the Chief Information Security Officer
**Applies to:** All employees, contractors, and temporary staff

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy governs the use of generative artificial intelligence tools by all
personnel of Northwind Financial Partners ("the Firm") in the course of their
work. It applies to any AI system that generates text, code, images, or analysis,
whether accessed through a browser, a desktop application, a mobile device, or an
integration embedded in another product.

This policy applies in addition to, and does not replace, the Firm's Information
Security Policy (NFP-IT-001), the Client Confidentiality Undertaking, and all
obligations arising under our regulatory permissions.

## 2. Approved Tools

2.1 Personnel may use only AI tools listed on the Approved AI Tools Register,
maintained by the CISO's office and published on the intranet.

2.2 Approved tools are provisioned through the Firm's enterprise accounts with
single sign-on. Personnel must not access an approved tool through a personal
account, a free tier, or any credential not issued by the Firm, as these
configurations do not carry the Firm's contractual data-protection terms.

2.3 Requests to add a tool to the Register must be submitted to the CISO's office
for security and vendor review. Trialling an unapproved tool with Firm data
before that review concludes is a breach of this policy, irrespective of whether
the trial is described as a pilot, an evaluation, or a proof of concept.

## 3. Client and Confidential Data

3.1 Personnel must not input client-identifying information into any AI tool.
This includes client names, account numbers, portfolio holdings, transaction
records, contact details, and any combination of details from which a client's
identity could reasonably be inferred.

3.2 The restriction in 3.1 applies regardless of the tool's stated data-retention
or training policy, and regardless of whether the tool is on the Approved AI
Tools Register. Approval of a tool addresses vendor security posture; it does not
grant permission to disclose client data to that vendor.

3.3 Where an AI tool would be genuinely useful for a task involving client
material, personnel must first remove or replace all identifying details so that
the remaining text could not be attributed to a specific client. Anonymised
material may then be processed using an approved tool.

3.4 Material that is confidential to the Firm but does not identify a client —
internal drafts, template documents, published research — may be processed using
an approved tool without anonymisation.

## 4. Prohibited Uses

4.1 AI tools must not be used to produce final client-facing advice,
recommendations, or valuations. AI output may inform an adviser's work; it may
not constitute the advice itself.

4.2 AI tools must not be used to generate or materially draft any document
submitted to a regulator, including regulatory correspondence, returns, and
attestations.

4.3 AI tools must not be used to circumvent a control, review step, or approval
that this or any other Firm policy requires.

## 5. Human Review and Accountability

5.1 Any AI-generated material used in the course of Firm business must be
reviewed by the responsible employee before use. The reviewing employee remains
fully accountable for the accuracy, completeness, and compliance of that
material. "The model produced it" is not a defence.

5.2 Where AI-generated material contains factual assertions, figures, citations,
or references to source documents, the responsible employee must verify each
against the underlying source before relying on it.

## 6. Disclosure to Clients

6.1 Where AI has been used materially in preparing a deliverable provided to a
client, that use must be disclosed to the client in accordance with the
Engagement Communications Standard.

6.2 Personnel must not represent AI-generated analysis as the independent work of
a named individual.

## 7. Incident Reporting

7.1 Any actual or suspected disclosure of client or confidential information to
an AI tool must be reported to the CISO's office within 24 hours of discovery.

7.2 Reports made promptly and in good faith under 7.1 will not by themselves
result in disciplinary action. Failure to report a known incident will.

7.3 Personnel who are unsure whether an intended use is permitted should consult
the CISO's office before proceeding, not after.

---

*Breach of this policy may result in disciplinary action up to and including
dismissal, and may constitute a breach of regulatory obligations.*
